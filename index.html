Dr.Babasaheb Ambedkar

Dr.Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar (1891-1956), widely known as Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar, was a preeminent Indian jurist, economist, social reformer, and statesman. He is most renowned as the chief architect of the Indian Constitution and is often referred to as the “Father of the Indian Constitution”. Ambedkar was also the first Law and Justice Minister of independent India. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar is my role model because he proved that knowledge, courage, and determination can break even the strongest chains of discrimination. Despite being born into a poor Dalit family and facing severe social injustice, he never gave up on his dreams. His deep love for education, his fight for equality, and his contribution to the Indian Constitution inspire me every day. He didn't just work for his own success—he dedicated his life to uplifting millions of others. His fearless attitude, sharp intellect, and commitment to social justice motivate me to study hard, stand up for what is right, and never be afraid of challenges. Like him, I want to make a positive difference in society.


Dr. A.P.J Abdul Kalam

Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam is my favorite person and also one of the most inspiring figures in Indian history. He was a brilliant scientist, a dedicated teacher, and the 11th President of India. Known as the “Missile Man of India,” he is remembered for his simplicity, intelligence, and love for the nation. Born on October 15, 1931, in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, Dr. Kalam came from a poor but honest family. Despite facing financial difficulties, he worked hard and pursued education with great determination. His journey from delivering newspapers to becoming a renowned scientist at ISRO and DRDO is truly motivational. Dr. Kalam played a key role in developing India's missile and space programs. He was also a key figure in India’s nuclear tests at Pokhran in 1998. Beyond his scientific achievements, what made him special was his humble personality, his connection with students, and his positive attitude toward life. He became the President of India in 2002 and was loved by people of all ages, especially children. Even after his presidency, he continued to travel, write books, and give lectures to students. His famous books like “Wings of Fire” and “Ignited Minds” inspire me to dream big and work hard. Dr. Kalam believed in the power of youth and always encouraged us to have dreams and never give up. His life teaches us that success is not about wealth or fame, but about dedication, honesty, and service to others. He passed away on July 27, 2015, while delivering a lecture to students—doing what he loved the most. Though he is no longer with us, his words and values will continue to guide me throughout my life. In conclusion, Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam is my favorite person because he showed the world that with strong willpower and hard work, anything is possible. He will always be my role model and a true hero of our nation.


Sindhutai Sapkal(Mother of Orphans)

One of the most inspiring people in my life is Sindhutai Sapkal. Known as the “Mother of Orphans,” she dedicated her entire life to helping abandoned and homeless children. Her story is not just touching, but also full of strength, struggle, and unconditional love. Sindhutai was born on November 14, 1948, in Wardha district, Maharashtra. She came from a very poor family and was married off at a very young age. Despite facing abuse, humiliation, and being thrown out of her house while pregnant, she never gave up. Instead of breaking down, she turned her pain into power. What makes her my favourite person is her unshakable courage and huge heart. She begged on the streets to feed not just herself, but other orphans she found along the way. Over time, she adopted more than 1,500 children and gave them food, shelter, education, and a loving home. She raised them like a true mother, and many of them are now doctors, lawyers, and responsible citizens. Her life proves that we don't need wealth to do something great. We just need a kind heart, strong willpower, and selfless love. She turned her personal suffering into a mission to bring joy into the lives of those who had no one. She even gave her own biological daughter to a trust so that she would not show any bias among her adopted children. Sindhutai received many awards, including the Padma Shri in 2021, but she always lived a simple life. Her speech, full of honesty and wisdom, always touched hearts. She passed away in January 2022, but her legacy will live forever. To me, Sindhutai Sapkal is more than a role model—she is a symbol of how one woman can change thousands of lives with love and sacrifice. She teaches me to be strong, generous, and to never lose hope, no matter how hard life becomes.


Swami Vivekananda

Among the many great personalities in history, Swami Vivekananda is one of my most favourite and inspiring figures. He was not only a great spiritual leader but also a symbol of strength, intelligence, and patriotism. His life, thoughts, and teachings have deeply influenced me and continue to guide me in my journey. Swami Vivekananda was born on January 12, 1863, in Kolkata, India. From a young age, he was intelligent, curious, and spiritual. He was a devoted disciple of Sri Ramakrishna Paramahamsa, and through his guidance, Vivekananda realized the importance of serving humanity as a form of worship. What makes Swami Vivekananda my favourite person is his powerful message to the youth. He always believed in the power of young minds and encouraged everyone to have confidence in themselves. His famous words, “Arise, awake, and stop not till the goal is reached,” give me motivation whenever I feel weak or lost. He represented India at the Parliament of the World's Religions in Chicago in 1893, where he introduced Hindu philosophy with the iconic speech that began with “Sisters and brothers of America.” His words were filled with wisdom, peace, and unity, and he earned global respect for India. I admire Swami Vivekananda not just for his knowledge, but for his boldness, clarity, and deep love for the nation. He always stood for truth, discipline, and service to society. His teachings encourage me to stay strong in my values, to never be afraid of failure, and to work for the betterment of others. Though he left the world at a young age of 39, his legacy lives on. His life inspires me to live with purpose, to believe in myself, and to be fearless in the face of challenges.
